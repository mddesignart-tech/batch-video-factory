RULES:
- Write in SIMPLE English. The audience are English learners, many of them
  Vietnamese. Short sentences. Common words. No idiomatic wordplay other than
  the idiom being taught.
- The comedy must be VISUAL. A viewer with the sound off should still laugh.
- Family friendly. Harmless cartoon slapstick only: nobody is hurt, nothing is
  destroyed in a way that would upset a child.
- FORBIDDEN: politics, religion, sexual content, graphic violence, hate speech,
  stereotypes, real people or celebrities, copyrighted characters, and anything
  that could be copied as a dangerous challenge.
- If the idiom has a violent literal reading (break a leg, bite the bullet, kick
  the bucket), stage it as soft cartoon comedy instead. Use the LITERAL VISUAL
  GAG above, which is already written safely.
- 4 to 6 scenes. Each scene 2-6 seconds, because each one is generated as a
  separate AI video clip.
- Prefer dialogue over narration. Keep every subtitle under 60 characters.
- Every scene needs a complete imagePrompt and videoPrompt that repeat the
  visual style and the character sheets verbatim, so the characters look the
  same in every shot.

CHARACTER LISTS - read this carefully, it is the most common mistake.

Every scene carries three separate lists. They are NOT the same list.

  "charactersPresent"
    EVERY character visible anywhere in the frame. Include a character who:
      - says nothing
      - stands or sits behind another character
      - only reacts, rolls their eyes, face-palms or laughs
      - walks past or enters the shot
      - holds, carries or hands over a prop
      - is only partly in frame (an arm, a shoulder, the back of a head)
    If a viewer would SEE them, they belong in this list.
    Do NOT list only the characters who speak. A character drawn without
    being listed here comes out looking like a different person.

  "speakingCharacters"
    Only characters with an actual spoken line in "dialogue". Often shorter
    than charactersPresent. May be empty for a silent visual beat.

  "primaryCharacters"
    The one or two characters the shot is actually about - whoever the camera
    is on. Usually one name.

Include a character only when the scene genuinely needs them. Two characters
carry most of these jokes. Do not add every available character to every scene:
each extra character costs another reference image and crowds the frame. A
third character should appear only where their reaction is part of the gag.

Consistency rules you must satisfy:
  - Everyone in speakingCharacters must also be in charactersPresent.
  - Everyone in primaryCharacters must also be in charactersPresent.
  - Every character named in "visualDescription", "characterAction" or
    "imagePrompt" must also be in charactersPresent.

Example of a scene where one character speaks and two are visible:
  "visualDescription": "Max panics at the door while Leo watches, arms folded.",
  "dialogue": "Max: \"I can't break a leg!\"",
  "charactersPresent": ["Max", "Leo"],
  "speakingCharacters": ["Max"],
  "primaryCharacters": ["Max"]

FRAMING - the image model renders a 2:3 portrait frame, which is WIDER than
the 9:16 video. The renderer scales to cover and centre-crops, so about 8% is
cut from the LEFT and 8% from the RIGHT; the full height is kept. So:
  - Keep every character, face, hand and important prop away from the left and
    right edges - inside the middle 84% of the width.
  - Do not spread two characters to opposite edges; keep them closer together.
  - Write imagePrompt for a vertical composition.

Set "complexity" per scene from how hard it is to generate:
  LOW    - one character present, little motion, simple background
  MEDIUM - two characters present, moderate motion or an important expression
  HIGH   - two or more characters interacting, hands, props, slapstick motion
  (count charactersPresent, not speakingCharacters)

Set "spendPriority" per scene:
  HIGH   - the first 3 seconds and the punchline
  NORMAL - ordinary story beats
  LOW    - static explanation or example cards

Set "angleKey" to a short kebab-case label for the comedy angle you chose
(for example "panic", "wrong-prop", "public-embarrassment").
